A journal entry can be just three lines. Give one ordinary moment a little space, then decide whether to leave it there. This worksheet works on paper, without Penumbra, an account, or JavaScript.

Keep it yours: Print this page or copy the questions into a notebook. There is no online entry box and nothing to submit here. Choose where to keep the paper, and leave names or identifying details out if you prefer.

Choose something manageable

Pick a recent, everyday moment: a changed plan, a small disappointment, a pleasant surprise, or a moment you felt at ease. You do not need to choose your hardest experience or explain your past.

You may skip any question, write a few words, or stop before writing anything. If reflecting brings up more than you want to sit with, put the page down and return to an ordinary activity or contact someone you trust.

Start with three lines

  1. What happened?

    Describe one moment in plain terms. What did you notice, without guessing what another person meant?

    ____________________________________________________________________

    ____________________________________________________________________

  2. What did I feel or notice?

    Use your own words for a feeling, or simply write “not sure.” If noticing a body sensation feels useful, you can add it; if not, leave that out.

    ____________________________________________________________________

    ____________________________________________________________________

  3. What would be useful now?

    Choose one small action, a pause, or no action. You do not have to solve the situation or turn it into a lesson.

    ____________________________________________________________________

    ____________________________________________________________________

A fictional, everyday example

What happened?
“A friend moved our Saturday coffee to next week. I had already put my shoes on.”
What did I feel or notice?
“Disappointed, then a little restless. I had been looking forward to getting out.”
What would be useful now?
“I can take a short walk and suggest a new time later. I do not need to decide what the change means about our friendship.”

This is one possible entry, not a model answer or an interpretation of your experience.

Optional: notice a little more

Only continue if you want to. One answer is enough, and leaving every line blank is also a choice.

  • Was there a small detail that steadied or lifted me?
  • What do I know for sure, and what am I assuming?
  • Is there a request or boundary I want to consider later, rather than act on now?
  • If I revisit this moment another day, what would I be curious to notice?

Close the page

Choose whether to keep the sheet, put it away, or discard it. Look around and choose an ordinary next step: have some water, look out a window, or return to what you were doing. There is no requirement to feel different after writing.

This worksheet is for self-reflection, not therapy, diagnosis, trauma processing, or medical care. You can stop or skip at any time. If you need support, reach out to a trusted person or a qualified professional. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. In the US, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; elsewhere, use a local crisis service.

Can't decide what to do tonight? Set a budget, a time window and indoors or out, and get one idea that fits your evening, from a one-song slow dance to a full-day wander. Not feeling it? Hit Another. You won't see the same idea twice until you've been through every match.

Pick tonight's date

    Ten to start with tonight

    • Chase the Sunset. Find the highest point you can reach on foot nearby and time your arrival for sundown, then narrate the colors to each other like sports commentators.
    • Living-Room Floor Picnic. Spread a blanket on the floor, plate up whatever is in the fridge, and eat by candlelight with both phones left in another room.
    • Star Map Backyard Lie-Down. Lie on a blanket outside after dark, use a stargazing app on one phone only, and try to find three constellations before you both get cold.
    • Pillow Fort Movie Marathon. Build the most ridiculous blanket fort your living room allows, then watch a trilogy inside it with only the snacks you can pass through the door flap.
    • Empty-the-Pantry Recipe Night. Pick a recipe using only ingredients already in your kitchen, cook it together with no recipe app open after step one, and name the dish yourselves.
    • Coin-Flip Wander. Flip a coin at your front door; heads you turn left, tails you turn right, and keep walking until something makes you both stop.
    • Slow Dance to One Song. Pick one song neither of you has heard in years, clear a patch of floor, and slow dance to it twice in a row.
    • Gratitude Jar Refill. Sit together and write ten new notes for your gratitude jar, one thing you appreciate about the other person on each slip.
    • Thrift Store Style Swap. Give each other a tiny budget and ten minutes in a secondhand shop to pick one item for the other to wear that day.
    • Library Card Cover Dare. Each pick a book for the other based only on the cover, then read the first chapter aloud to each other that night.

    Making date night actually happen

    Most couples don't lack ideas. They lack a decision. The picker above removes that step: you decide only how much money and time you have, then you say yes to the first idea that doesn't make either of you wince.

    Choose in under two minutes

    1. Start with the constraints. What is the budget tonight, how long do you really have, and do you both have the energy to leave the house?
    2. Draw one idea each. Each of you taps Show me an idea. If one lands, you're done. If both do, flip a coin.
    3. Veto a few times. Either of you can press Another, but only a few times. If nothing sticks after three, loosen one filter to Any.
    4. Commit out loud. Say the start time and who sets up. An idea without a time slips to next week.

    Budget without the awkwardness

    • Free ideas cost nothing but attention: a walk with a rule, a floor picnic, a gratitude exchange.
    • Cheap means a few dollars: change for a vending machine, a secondhand shop, a pot of tea.
    • Splurge is the occasional treat. Agree on a ceiling before you start, not at the bill.
    • Alternate: a free night most weeks keeps a splurge night feeling like an event.

    Keep it regular

    Put a recurring slot on both calendars, even a short one, and treat it like an appointment. A fixed night means you're choosing what to do, not whether to.

    • Take turns being the planner. Whoever plans picks the idea, the other just shows up.
    • Phones in another room for the first half hour.
    • When a night flops, keep the idea and change the setting rather than dropping the habit.
    • If the week falls apart, shrink the date to twenty minutes instead of canceling it.
